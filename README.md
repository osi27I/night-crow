# THE NIGHT CROW

A 3D stealth story, written entirely in **Python** with the **Ursina** engine (which sits on top of Panda3D).
CT029-3-2 Imaging and Special Effects, group assignment, Phase 2.

The game follows Abdo bin Suhail, a farmer who is pushed into an assassination, loses everything, and becomes the
**Night Crow**. It now has six playable scenes (Scene 4 is split into two missions and there is a finale with a boss fight).
A full play-through takes roughly 25 to 40 minutes the first time (an estimate from reading and bot-testing, not from a human play-through; time it yourselves).

Every model in this game is built from cubes, spheres and cylinders in code, every texture is drawn by
a script, every sound and every piece of music is generated by a script. There are no downloaded
assets and no external art files, so there is nothing to credit and nothing to lose. (The one exception is the
DejaVu Serif font in `assets/fonts`, which is free to use; its licence file is next to it.)

## 1. Run it

```
pip install -r requirements.txt        (or just:  pip install ursina==8.3.0)
python main.py
```

Developed and tested with Python 3.13, Ursina 8.3.0 and Panda3D 1.10.16 (other Python versions that Ursina supports should work, but only 3.13 was tried).
Pin that version: Ursina changes between releases, and 8.3.0 is the one every file here was tested on.

## 2. How to play

| key              | action                                                              |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| W A S D          | walk                                                                |
| Shift            | run (louder, guards hear you close by)                              |
| E                | interact (the prompt appears at the bottom of the screen)           |
| Space            | advance dialogue / silence a guard from behind                      |
| 1 to 4           | pick an answer when a choice appears                                |
| Q (Night Crow)   | CAW: throw a crow's call ahead of you, nearby guards go to look     |

**Extra controls that appear in particular scenes**

| key / action                | where                  | what it does |
|-----------------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| A / D (or the arrow keys)   | Scene 1, the scroll    | turn the cipher wheel until the message makes sense |
| Space                       | Scene 3, the hunt      | shoot the bow at a hare in front of you (walk, do not run) |
| C                           | Scenes 4 to 6          | **Crow Sight**: 6 seconds of vision that shows guards, loot, lights and the objective through walls |
| G                           | Scenes 4 to 6          | **Smoke bomb**: a cloud that blocks every guard's sight and lets you silence one from any side |
| R                           | Scenes 4 to 6          | **Throwing knife**: kills an unarmoured guard at range, armoured guards only look round |
| E on a ladder, push at an edge | Scene 5 (rooftops)  | climb to the roofs, leap from roof to roof, Space on a roof drops onto a guard below |
| Space / F / R / G           | Scene 6 (boss fight)   | strike / dodge roll / knife / smoke bomb |

The camera follows Abdo from above and behind. In the stealth scenes every guard has a glowing cone on
the floor: green = calm, red = he has noticed you. **Guards see you less in the dark**, so put the torches out.

## 4. Where everything lives

```
main.py                 start-up and command-line options
engine/                 everything shared by the four scenes
    game.py             window, scene switching, global keys
    scene_base.py       parent class of every scene (camera, cut-scene helpers, caught/retry)
    shaders.py          the GLSL shaders: lit_shader (custom lighting + fog) and post_shader (bloom, vignette ...)
    lighting.py         LightRig: up to 8 coloured, flickering point lights fed to the shader
    postfx.py           tweenable full-screen effects (bloom, flash, desaturation, chromatic aberration ...)
    particles.py        our own pooled particle system: fire, smoke, embers, sparks, dust, magic
    characters.py       procedural people (head with neck/eyes/brows/nose, robe, arms, legs, head coverings and crown, weapons, skins) and their poses
    ai.py               Guard (vision cone, light-based detection, patrol, hearing, blindness, armour, rooftop archers) and Follower
    crow.py             Crow Sight, smoke bomb, throwing knife, supply pickups and the rooftop system (roofs, ladders, leaps)
    batch.py            merges thousands of small static objects into a few big meshes after a scene is built (this is the main speed-up).
                        It first strips Ursina's per-entity shader inputs, otherwise Panda cannot join meshes of different containers
    collision.py        walls, boxes, circles and line-of-sight tests
    player.py           WASD movement, running, footsteps
    camera_rig.py       follow camera, cinematic shots, shake, FOV
    timing.py           Timeline: tweens, timers, easing, and generator-based cut-scenes
    hud.py, dialogue.py captions, title cards, prompts, objectives, dialogue box with typewriter text
    audio.py            sounds, loops, music with fades
    props.py            torches, braziers, walls, houses (with doors), furniture, rugs, thrones, palms, bushes, hedges, crop fields,
                        fire logs and seats, tents, wall sconces, hanging rope, banners, the crow
    entities.py         the shared 3D shapes (cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus, leaf blade) built by code, and the mk() helper
scenes/                 one file per member
tools/
    make_textures.py    draws every texture in assets/textures
    make_audio.py       synthesises every sound and every music loop in assets/sounds and assets/music
    playtest.py         runs the game headless and takes screenshots (how it was tested)
    smoke_test.py       boots one scene and then the next one and prints errors and frame time
assets/                 textures (PNG), sounds and music (WAV), fonts
```

## 5. Special effects, and where to find them in the code (for the reports)

**Rendering**
* Custom lighting shader `lit_shader` in `engine/shaders.py`: ambient + moon/sun + up to 8 coloured point
  lights with smooth fall-off, distance fog and a per-object "emissive" glow. Written in GLSL.
* Full-screen post-process `post_shader`: bloom, vignette, desaturation, film grain, chromatic aberration
  and colour grading, all animated at runtime from `engine/postfx.py` (`game.post.tween(...)`).
* Pooled billboard particle system (`engine/particles.py`): fire, embers, smoke, sparks, dust, magic.
* Glow sprites, soft ground shadows, sky dome with stars, additive moon halo (`engine/props.py`).
* Procedural 3D models built vertex by vertex in `engine/entities.py`: sphere (with automatic lower detail for small ones),
  cylinder, cone, torus and a leaf blade. They are built with the winding order the game's left-handed set-up needs, so they are
  solid from outside (an earlier version was inside-out, which made hay, horses, helmets and heads look see-through).
* Plants (`engine/props.py`): `palm()` (10 fronds with real leaflets, date bunches on stalks, sway animation), `bush()`,
  `hedge()` / `hedge_box()` (seamless `leaves.png` texture), `crop_field()` (furrows with leafy plants and golden ears).
* Camp and set dressing: `fire_logs()` (ash bed, charred logs, embers, stone ring), `seat_mat()` and `log_seat()`, `rug()` (lifted
  and depth-offset so it never z-fights the floor), `throne()`, `hanging_rope()`, `wall_sconce()` (torch holder on a wall, light in
  front of it), `tent()` and `tent_flap()` (a cloth door that opens), `house(..., door_cover="plank")` (closed door with collision).

**Animation**
* Everything moves from code: characters walk, sit, kneel, fall and lie down by rotating joints with sine
  waves (`engine/characters.py`). Banners sway, torches flicker, crows flap their wings.
* `Timeline` (`engine/timing.py`) tweens any number or colour with easing, and cut-scenes are written as
  Python generators (`yield 2.0` waits two seconds). This is how the cinematic shots are scripted.
* Slow motion with `Clock.time_scale`, screen shake, camera shots and letterbox bars.

**Gameplay systems**
* Stealth: field-of-view cone, line-of-sight tests against walls, an awareness meter, and detection that
  depends on how brightly lit the player is, using the same light values the shader draws (`engine/ai.py`).
* Hearing and distraction: `Guard.hear()` sends a guard to investigate a noise (Scene 4's crow call).
* Takedown from behind: the rear arc test is `Guard.is_behind()`.

## 6. Changing things

* **Replace a sound or texture**: put your own file in `assets/sounds`, `assets/music` or `assets/textures`
  with the *same name*. The game picks it up with no code change. A missing file is simply skipped.
* **Regenerate the generated assets**: `python tools/make_textures.py` and `python tools/make_audio.py`
  (the second one accepts names: `python tools/make_audio.py caw mus_crow`). Needs numpy, scipy and pillow.
* **Rename a character or change a line**: dialogue lines are plain Python tuples `("Name", "text")` inside
  each scene's cut-scene functions. Speaker colours are in `engine/dialogue.py` (`SPEAKER_COLORS`).
* **Make a scene easier or harder**: guard `view`, `fov`, `speed` and `alert_mult` are arguments of `Guard(...)`
  where each scene creates its guards.
* **Add a new scene**: copy any scene file, change the class name, and add it to `_REGISTRY` in
  `scenes/__init__.py`. If one scene file has an error the others still run.

## 7. Known limitations (read before you demo)

* All of the automated testing ran with software rendering at a few frames per second. Static scenery is merged into
  a few big meshes (see `engine/batch.py`). After the last fix a scene sends roughly 500 to 1,300 meshes to the graphics card
  per frame instead of 3,000 to 7,600, but **nobody has timed it on a real graphics card yet**. Run `python main.py --fps` on the
  machine you will present on. If it is slow press **F10** (performance mode: no bloom glow, half the particles) or start with `--safe`.
* The sounds and music are synthesised. They were checked for clipping and level by script, but
  please listen to them once with headphones before recording. Replace anything you do not like.
* The stealth scenes and the boss fight were played by scripts (bots), not by people. Guard speed, vision ranges and the
  boss's health and damage may need a tweak after a real play-through. The boss's numbers are constants at the top of
  `Marcellus` in `scenes/scene5_finale.py`.
* Names such as Harith, Qais, Jabir, Rashid, Fawaz, Centurion Varro, Prefect Marcellus and the city of Antioch are
  invented for this game and can be changed freely.
* Scene 3 now starts with a hunt on a separate patch of ground far to the west (so the burning village is not visible early),
  and the second title card changed from "THREE DAYS LATER" to "THAT EVENING". Scenes 5 and 6 are new work that was not part
  of the original scene split, so decide who presents them.
* Houses, bodies, horses, palms and rocks are built from many more parts than before (the palms were slimmed down and small
  spheres use less detail to keep the triangle count in check). On the software-rendered test machine frame time rose a little
  compared with the previous build (for example Scene 1 about 230 ms vs 175 ms per frame, Scene 3 about 320-355 vs 290), which
  says little about a real graphics card. If the frame rate drops in Scene 3, try `--safe` first.
* The Phase 1 document still describes a 2D Arcade game. Phase 2 uses Ursina and 3D, so mention the change
  of tools in the report introduction.
